# Graphic language

## Stable grammar

- Build one decisive event against one quieter field. Calm does not mean uniformly small, pale, or empty.
- Prefer continuity, compression, layering, seams, folds, traces of use, and poised material depth over floating tech shapes.
- Use warm stone, cotton paper, vellum, linen, wood, soft translucent film, and quiet daylight only when they express capture, record, structure, or connection.
- A line or seam must encode a named transition. If nothing changes along it, remove it.
- Preserve irregular real-world cropping and in-between gestures in photo work. Do not turn people into centered campaign models.
- Keep people warm, contemporary, and professionally plausible without boardroom staging. Favor mixed groups, indirect gaze, partial faces, natural motion, and shared attention.

## Tiro-specific routes

### Product proof

The verified product fragment leads. Graphic material may frame, crop, reveal, or connect it; never redraw or sanitize it into a generic interface.

### Conversation moment

The human interaction leads. Tiro specificity comes from the later message and a real record/capture role, not from a staged laptop meeting or generic coworking scene.

### Nib-derived structure

The official nib may be introduced later as an exact vector layer. Raw generated material may echo compression, a base stroke plus modifier, or a converging record structure, but must not imitate or distort the logo.

### Record compression

Several transient fragments resolve into one durable structure. Use material relationships rather than explanatory nodes, fake text, or decorative paths.

## Failure signals

- generic headline-left / complete-image-right split with no causal relationship;
- equal cards, floating nodes, glow, gradient orb, or arbitrary translucent panels;
- decorative orange rules or traces with no semantic transition;
- fake UI, invented labels, pseudo-writing, certification seals, or metrics;
- pastoral/Kinfolk atmosphere without an observable record or connection role;
- complete stock-photo people facing camera or exaggerated office enthusiasm;
- a logo-like generated mark that competes with the official vector asset;
- no focal event at thumbnail size or no usable quiet zone for later composition.
