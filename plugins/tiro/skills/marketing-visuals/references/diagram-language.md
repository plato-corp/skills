# Diagram language

Use when an external article image must explain a relationship rather than establish only mood or announcement emphasis.

## Representation gate

Choose the medium from the viewer's required understanding.

| Viewer must understand | Default representation |
|---|---|
| Event, promise, or emotional emphasis | Image-led hero, official nib, or brand metaphor |
| Hierarchy or one source producing several outcomes | Result tree or branching map |
| Containment, ownership, permission, or visibility | Nested boundary diagram |
| Movement between spaces or states | Transition map |
| Ordered process or transformation | Process rail |
| Difference between two real models | Comparison structure |
| A capability proven by the interface | Verified product fragment with restrained annotation |

A photograph is not evidence of hierarchy, transition, ownership, or visibility. A diagram is not needed when one verified phrase, object, or product fragment already proves the message more directly.

## Assign one job to every frame

Before styling a multi-frame article, write the sequence as communication roles:

```text
Hero / promise → relationship or mechanism → transition → boundary or access → product proof
```

Not every article uses every role. A frame receives one dominant job. Series consistency comes from type roles, margins, palette, line weight, step notation, and brand signature—not from forcing every frame to use the same medium.

For a workspace article, a useful route is:

- Hero: announce that Tiro has expanded for teams.
- Company policy: show one company workspace producing asset retention, permission management, and unified billing.
- Workspace transition: show one account branching into company, project, and personal contexts.
- Personal visibility: show shared and private records as different visibility regions inside the same team workspace.

## Build from verified structure

Before drawing, record:

```text
Claim: <one relationship the frame proves>
Entities: <real nouns>
Connections: <what each line means>
Boundaries: <inside/outside, shared/private, parent/child>
States or order: <only when the claim has them>
Unknowns: <facts that must not be invented>
```

Choose the smallest grammar that preserves those facts. If the relationship cannot be described in one sentence, split the frame or clarify the message before adding nodes.

## Tiro-specific diagram behavior

- The official nib may act as a source, actor, account root, or record origin when that role is true.
- A trace may branch, cross a boundary, or change form only when the visual change maps to a named product relationship.
- Use grouping and spatial boundaries before containers, dividers before fills, and fills before shadows. Diagrams should remain legible without decorative depth.
- Keep labels concrete and editable. Prefer `회사 워크스페이스`, `공유 기록`, and `나에게만 보임` over abstract labels such as `Node A` or unexplained English metadata.
- Use actual product UI only when the current verified screen is available. Otherwise show the concept as a diagram and label unresolved behavior for verification rather than inventing an interface.

## Avoid diagram theater

- equal cards when the entities are not equal;
- repeated nodes that do not change the viewer's understanding;
- connectors with no named meaning;
- fake dashboards, sanitized product screens, or invented controls;
- shrinking an entire product model into unreadable labels;
- copying the hero's photo treatment into frames whose purpose is explanation;
- adding a diagram merely to make an image appear technical.

## Complete-frame contract

Every explainer frame includes an editable headline, support line, diagram, and restrained Tiro signature. The diagram must answer its claim at thumbnail scale before body copy is read.

Before release, verify:

- a viewer can state the relationship represented by the frame;
- every line, branch, group, and boundary has a named meaning;
- the diagram uses only verified entities and behavior;
- no text overlaps another label or line, and no text exceeds its frame;
- every connector visibly terminates at its intended boundary without protruding through a node or label;
- type size, type weight, stroke weight, and spacing belong to one visual hierarchy rather than competing for attention;
- the diagram has been inspected at thumbnail scale and at actual export size; node counts, grid alignment, and other numeric checks are not substitutes for this visual review;
- any editorial gesture added only to make the diagram feel distinctive is removed when it weakens reading order or fit;
- the series remains coherent even though its media differ;
- Korean labels use word-based wrapping and zero or negative tracking;
- the weakest critical quality score is at least 9.0.
